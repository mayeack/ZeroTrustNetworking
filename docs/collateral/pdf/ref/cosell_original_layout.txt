workload instead of the rack, and routing at the edge so a new site comes up in days. Cisco owns the enforcement points: the Linux kernel through
Isovalent (Cilium and Tetragon), the NIC and DPU through Hybrid Mesh Firewall and Hypershield, and the switch through Nexus and the N9300 Smart
Switches, with Live Protect shielding the switch OS. Splunk owns the proof: every flow and every policy decision lands in the platform the customer
already runs, Enterprise Security turns them into findings, and SOAR and Agent Launchpad push the decision back to the enforcement point. Sell the
loop, not the parts.

The integration exists today; sell it with confidence. Hubble Enterprise exports flow logs to the Splunk HTTP Event Collector. The Cisco Security Cloud
App for Splunk (Splunkbase 7404) parses Cisco Isovalent Runtime Security events from Tetragon, and the Splunk Threat Research Team shipped
Kubernetes detections on that data through the Enterprise Security Content Update in February 2026. Splunk Observability Cloud's Network Explorer runs
on Tetragon and Cilium. Nexus data arrives through the Cisco-built data center networking add-on, and Live Protect exports to Splunk from Nexus
Dashboard. The gap to close in the cycle is the job-to-fabric join (Slurm or Kubernetes job ID to switch port) and the SOAR connectors for Hypershield
and Nexus; scope both into the lab phase.
Co-sell roles, one story. The Cisco seller leads with the enforcement plane: Isovalent, Nexus and the N9300 Smart Switches, Hypershield, Live Protect,
and the Nexus One fabric integration that carries Security Group Tags from Kubernetes into the switch. The Splunk SE leads with the loop: observe,
decide, enforce, verify; the three dashboards; SOAR plus Agent Launchpad. Two buyers in the room: the security lead (identity, enforcement, evidence)
and the network lead (segmentation, routing at the edge). The AI infrastructure lead is the third, and efficiency is the word: every GPU-hour waiting on the
fabric is paid for.
Competitive frame. Against a network-detection incumbent: it sees mirrored traffic, not workload identity or the process behind a connection, and it
cannot enforce in the east-west path; keep it for north-south anomaly detection and land its alerts in Splunk too. Against Calico: identity-based policy at
L3 to L7, Hubble and Tetragon observability, process-aware enforcement, VMs under the same model, and Security Group Tag propagation into the Nexus
fabric, with Cisco engineering and support behind it. Against "we'll run open-source Cilium ourselves": Isovalent Enterprise adds Tetragon enterprise,
Timescape, SIEM export, 24x7 support and the Cisco fabric integration.
